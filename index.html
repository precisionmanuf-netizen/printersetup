Printer Not Working After Changing Wi-Fi Password? How to Reconnect

Changed your Wi-Fi password and now your wireless printer won't connect or shows "Printer Offline"? Don't worry—your printer hardware is completely fine. When you update your router password, printers cannot update automatically like phones or laptops do. Follow this clear, step-by-step guide to reconnect your HP, Canon, Brother, or Epson printer to your new wireless network in just a few minutes.

Fast Solution

In a Rush? Here is the 60-Second Reconnection Fix

If you don't have time to read the full guide, perform these 3 universal actions immediately:

  1. Printers with Touchscreens: Tap Settings (Gear) > Network Setup > Wireless Settings > Wireless Setup Wizard. Pick your Wi-Fi name and type your new password.
  2. Printers with Buttons Only (No Screen): Hold the Wi-Fi button on your printer for 4 seconds until it blinks. Within 2 minutes, push the WPS button on your Wi-Fi router. The printer connects automatically.
  3. Reboot Both: Power-cycle your router and printer. If your PC still says "Offline", remove the printer in Windows Settings / Mac System Settings and click Add Device.

Updating your home or office Wi-Fi password is one of the most essential cybersecurity practices. It prevents unauthorized neighbors from leeching your broadband, locks out unauthorized IoT gadgets, and shields confidential personal files from intrusion. But almost universally, the moment that new passphrase takes effect, a familiar household headache strikes: your wireless printer vanishes from your network and refuses to print.

Whether you are printing urgent tax filings, children's school assignments, or contracts for a remote client, receiving the dreaded "Printer Offline" or "Device Communication Error" stops your daily schedule in its tracks. While smartphones and laptops immediately prompt you with a friendly password box, printers stay stubbornly mute.

Here is the good news: your printer hardware is completely fine. It did not break, and its wireless card has not failed. It is simply trapped in a handshake mismatch. With the right protocol, you will restore your device back to full functionality. Why Printers Disconnect When Wi-Fi Passwords Change

To troubleshoot effectively, we must look at how network authentication operates beneath the surface. Operating systems like Windows 11, macOS Sequoia, iOS, and Android feature continuous visual listener interfaces. When an SSID rejects old credentials, the OS recognizes the 4-Way Handshake Failure (Deauth Frame 802.11) and presents you with an instant dialog box asking for the updated key.

Printers operate under very different constraints. A printer relies on stripped-down, lightweight microcontroller firmware designed for low-power operation. When you initially learned How to Install a Printer, your printer permanently burned the following variables into its non-volatile EEPROM/NVRAM chip:

  • Target Service Set Identifier (SSID): The broadcast name of your router.
  • Security Encryption Standard: Usually WPA2-PSK (AES) or WPA3-Personal.
  • Pre-Shared Security Key (PSK): The exact alphanumeric password.
  • Assigned Local IPv4 Lease: The dynamic DHCP IP address issued by your router.

Crucial Insight for Home Users: Even if your Wi-Fi network name (SSID) stayed 100% identical, changing even one letter or number of the password invalidates the printer's stored encryption token. It will never guess or refresh the password on its own.

4 Master Methods to Reconnect Any Wireless Printer

Technicians use four reliable techniques to push new network credentials into a printer. Depending on whether your hardware has a touchscreen, physical buttons, or a USB port, choose the method that best matches your setup:

1

The On-Screen Wireless Setup Wizard (Touchscreens & LCDs)

This is the fastest, cleanest approach. If your printer has a digital display, follow these steps right on the device:

  1. Power on the printer and tap the Wireless or Settings icon (often a gear or network symbol).
  2. Navigate to Network Setup > Wireless Settings > Wireless Setup Wizard.
  3. The printer scans your local 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz airwaves. Select your home network SSID from the detected list.
  4. Carefully enter your new Wi-Fi password on the touchscreen keypad. Passwords are case-sensitive.
  5. Tap Done or OK. The Wi-Fi LED will blink while negotiating, then turn solid blue or green to confirm pairing.
2

The WPS Push-Button Method (Screenless Models)

If your printer lacks an LCD display and only has tactile buttons, WPS (Wi-Fi Protected Setup) allows hardware-level pairing:

  1. Confirm your Wi-Fi router has a physical WPS button (look for a pair of curved arrows).
  2. Press and hold the Wi-Fi button on your printer for 3 to 5 seconds until the wireless light blinks rapidly.
  3. Within 2 minutes, walk to your router and hold down its physical WPS button for 3 seconds.
  4. The router and printer exchange encrypted cryptographic keys automatically. When the light stops flashing and stays solid, you are connected.
3

Smartphone Companion Apps (HP Smart, Canon PRINT, Epson Smart Panel)

Modern printers support smartphone configuration using Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE):

  1. Put the printer into Wi-Fi Setup Mode: For screenless HP printers, hold Wireless + Cancel for 5 seconds. On Canon models, hold the Direct or Wi-Fi button.
  2. Open your smartphone's official manufacturer app (e.g., HP Smart, Canon PRINT Inkjet/SELPHY, Brother Mobile Connect, or Epson Smart Panel).
  3. Tap Add Printer or the + symbol. The app discovers the beaconing printer.
  4. Select your main Wi-Fi network and enter your new password. The app uploads the credentials directly into the printer's board.
4

Direct USB Reconfiguration via Laptop / PC

If your router has WPS disabled and your printer lacks a screen, use a standard USB cable (Type-A to Type-B) to push settings:

  1. Plug the USB cable between your Windows PC or Mac and the printer.
  2. Open your printer's desktop software utility (e.g., Brother iPrint&Scan or Canon IJ Network Device Setup Utility).
  3. Choose Wireless Device Setup or Convert USB to Wireless Connection.
  4. The software detects your printer over USB, asks for your new Wi-Fi password, and saves it into the printer memory.
  5. Unplug the USB cable once complete. Your wireless printer is ready to use.

Brand-Specific Instructions: HP, Canon, Brother & Epson

Different manufacturers use different button combinations, menu layouts, and reset sequences. Click on your printer's brand below to view exact, model-specific instructions:

HP Wireless Reconnection Walkthrough

HP printers frequently enter an "E9" or "Offline" state after router changes. Here is how to clear it:

  • HP Models with Touchscreen (Envy Inspire, OfficeJet Pro 8000/9000): Swipe down from the top of the display to access the Control Dashboard > Tap Setup (Gear) > Network Setup > Restore Network Settings. Confirm Yes. Once restored, select Wireless Setup Wizard and select your updated network.
  • HP DeskJet 2700 / 3700 / Envy 6000 (No Screen): Locate the Wireless and Cancel (X) buttons. Hold them both down together for 5 seconds until the power button pulses. Open the HP Smart app on your PC or phone, click Set Up a New Printer, and supply your new password.

Canon PIXMA & MAXIFY Reconnection Walkthrough

Canon utilizes an intelligent "Cableless Setup" mode designed for straightforward mobile matching:

  • Canon Models with Color Display: Press the Setup button (represented by a wrench/screwdriver) > Navigate to Device Settings > LAN Settings > Wi-Fi > Manual Connect. Choose your router name and enter the security passphrase.
  • Canon PIXMA TS / MG Series (No Screen): Hold down the Wireless Connect button (or Wi-Fi button) for 3 seconds until the orange and blue LEDs alternate. Open the Canon PRINT Inkjet/SELPHY app, tap Register Printer, and complete the setup wizard.

Brother Monochrome & Color Reconnection Walkthrough

Brother laser and inkjet printers feature very consistent menu structures across their lineup:

  • Press the physical Menu or Settings button on the control panel.
  • Use the arrow keys to scroll down to Network > press OK.
  • Select WLAN > press OK > Select Setup Wizard > press OK.
  • When prompted "WLAN Enable?", select Yes (or press 1).
  • Select your SSID from the list, enter your new Wi-Fi password using the keypad, and press OK. Brother will automatically print an informative WLAN Report showing "Connection: OK".

Epson EcoTank & WorkForce Reconnection Walkthrough

Epson EcoTank models make wireless recovery straightforward via their home screen interface:

  • On the printer display, tap or scroll to Settings > Network Settings > Wi-Fi Setup.
  • Choose Wi-Fi (Recommended) > Wi-Fi Setup Wizard.
  • Pick your network SSID from the displayed list.
  • Input your new Wi-Fi password carefully. Tap Done > Start Setup. The top-left Wi-Fi symbol will show solid bars once connected.

Resolving Driver & Spooler Glitches After Reconnection

What happens if your printer shows a solid Wi-Fi light, but your computer still reports "Printer Offline"? This is one of the most common issues after changing Wi-Fi settings.

The underlying cause is almost always an internal IP address mismatch. When your router rebooted with its new password, its DHCP server issued a brand-new internal local IP address (for example, switching your printer from 192.168.1.15 to 192.168.1.72). However, your operating system's print queue is still trying to send documents to the old, unassigned IP address.

Observed Symptom Root Cause Analysis Immediate Action Step
Solid Wi-Fi LED, but PC says "Offline" Print spooler port pointing to outdated local IP Remove printer in OS Settings and click Add Device
Print jobs stuck in queue (Error) Corrupted Windows Print Spooler buffer files Restart the spoolsv.exe service via terminal/services
Cannot see 5GHz Wi-Fi network name Printer wireless card only supports 2.4GHz Split router bands into separate 2.4GHz and 5GHz SSIDs
Prompts for "Printer PIN" during setup Wi-Fi Direct security passcode enabled Check serial sticker on back or print Network Config sheet

Quick Windows Spooler Reset Command

If your print queue is locked up with unresponsive jobs, open Windows Command Prompt as Administrator and run this command:

net stop spooler && del /Q /F /S "%systemroot%\System32\Spool\Printers\*.*" && net start spooler

If the communication bridge between your computer and the printer remains disrupted, you may need to update or install printer drivers. Fresh manufacturer drivers update your computer's discovery protocols (such as WSD and mDNS/Bonjour), allowing automatic detection of your printer even if its local IP changes.

3 Best Practices to Prevent Disconnections in the Future

Rather than having to reconfigure your printer every time your router updates or reboots, network engineers use three proven strategies to maintain a permanent connection:

1. Set Up a DHCP IP Reservation

By default, home routers lease temporary dynamic IP addresses that expire every 24 to 72 hours. By logging into your router's administration portal (typically at 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1) and navigating to LAN Settings > DHCP Reservation, you can bind your printer’s permanent physical MAC address to a fixed IP. Your printer will then always receive the exact same IP address, preventing connection dropouts.

2. Maintain a Dedicated 2.4 GHz Smart Home / IoT Network

Modern mesh routers (e.g., Eero, Netgear Orbi, Google Nest Wi-Fi) combine both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands under a single name using "band steering." Because older printer transceivers cannot understand 5 GHz signals, they often disconnect when the router tries to steer them. Creating a dedicated 2.4 GHz network with its own password gives your printer a stable, dedicated channel.

3. Disable "SNMP Status Enabled" in Windows Port Settings

Windows uses Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP) to monitor ink and paper levels. If your router blocks SNMP packets across subnets, Windows falsely flags the printer as offline. In Windows, go to Printers > Printer Properties > Ports > Configure Port, and uncheck SNMP Status Enabled.
